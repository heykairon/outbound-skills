# Benchmarks

Every number below comes from campaigns run through [Kairon](https://heykairon.com). They are our own sends, not vendor surveys. Treat them as where good campaigns landed, not as targets to hit. Compare yourself against your own baseline first.

Last updated: 2026-11-09. The funnel rates were re-measured on 2026-11-06. What moves acceptance and what moves interest in the messages were measured on 2026-11-09. Earlier sections on warm-up, the note, the first message and follow-ups were removed: the re-measured data did not support them.

## Funnel rates across clients

Measured on 48,843 invitations from 52 organizations that sent at least 300 each, June to November 2026. Each rate is the median organization: half of our clients do better, half do worse. Each one follows the same people from the invitation on, so multiplying the steps gives about the same result as the last two rows.

| Stage | Typical organization |
|-------|----------------------|
| Invitation → accepted | 33% |
| Accepted → replied | 26% |
| Replied → interested | 26% |
| Interested → meeting booked | ~50% (assumption) |
| Invitation → interested | 2.0% |
| Invitation → meeting booked | ~1% (assumption) |

- **Replied** means they answered any message in the sequence.
- **Interested** means the reply was classified positive: they want the thing offered, or a call.
- **Meeting booked is an assumption, not a measurement.** An interested reply shows explicit interest: the person asks for the call or sends their contact, so we assume about half of them end in a meeting. What we can measure is only a floor. A meeting counts when someone moves the lead to "meeting booked" or "won", and 20 of the 52 organizations never do. Among the 32 that do, 17% of interested replies and 0.4% of invitations end in a marked meeting.

The spread between organizations is wide. Compare yourself against your own numbers first.

Platform ceiling: about 150 connection invitations per seat per week.

## What moves acceptance

Measured on 44,396 invitations from 96 sender accounts in 64 organizations, June to November 2026. Each number compares invitations sent by the same account, so the sender's offer, market and profile stay the same. Mutual connections were measured on 1,927 of them, and the recipient's activity on 999. These are associations, not experiments.

| The recipient | Change in acceptance | Against |
|---------------|----------------------|---------|
| 1–2 mutual connections | +15 points | none (3rd degree accepted 6%) |
| 3–10 mutual connections | +26 points | none |
| 11–30 mutual connections | +32 points | none |
| 31–100 mutual connections | +46 points | none |
| 100+ mutual connections | +57 points | none |
| Published at least one post in the 30 days before | +12 points | no post |
| 3–10 posts, reposts or comments in the 30 days before | +14 points | no activity (59% of people, who accepted 21%) |
| Started the current position 0–3 months ago | +10 points | 1–3 years in it |
| 3,000+ connections, with mutual connections held the same | +19 points | under 500 |
| C-level or founder | −10 points | individual contributor |
| Director or manager | −6 points | individual contributor |
| More than 25 years of career | −12 points | 8–15 years |

| The note | Change in acceptance | Against |
|----------|----------------------|---------|
| Pitches the offer, or asks for a meeting, a call or an event | −6 points | no note |
| Gives a reason about the recipient (a post, a school, an event) | +5 points, not significant | no note |

Warming up before the invitation (a visit, a like) did not raise acceptance: within the same account it was 3 points lower, not significant.

## What moves interest in the messages

Measured on 12,553 people who got at least one message, in 200 campaigns from 75 sender accounts in 59 organizations, July to November 2026. Recruiting and career coaching are left out: there the buyer is a person, not a business. Each number compares messages sent by the same account. Across them, 6.6% replied with interest. These are associations, not experiments.

| What the first message offers | Interested | Within the same account, against a product pitch |
|-------------------------------|-----------|---------------------------------------------------|
| A partnership or a commission | 23.6% | +20 points (2 accounts) |
| A request for help or an opinion | 11.4% | +9 points (1 account) |
| An online event with a date | 8.8% | +5 points (3 accounts) |
| Something made for this person | 8.5% (the typical campaign 1.9%) | no clear difference |
| An in-person event | 7.8% | +6 points (1 account) |
| A product pitch | 6.2% | reference |
| A trip to their city | 5.5% | no clear difference |
| A resource that is the same for everyone | 4.8% | no clear difference |
| Only a question about them | 4.4% | no clear difference |

Things made for the person worked when they were already made and needed only a yes: a video prepared for their company, 22.8% of 219. A free diagnosis that needed a 45-minute call drew 4–6%. Trips worked when tied to an event the person could attend: a stand at a tech week, 12.3% of 65. Trips that only named a city and dates drew 0–3.6%.

| The rest of the message | Change in interest | Against |
|-------------------------|--------------------|---------|
| A reason specific to this person | +3.6 points | a reason not specific to them (20 accounts); against no reason, +2.5 to +3.1, not significant |
| A generic reason, or the sender's own plans | no difference | no reason |
| A link or a form in the first message | −3.9 points | none |
| Ends with a question about their problem or how they work | −7.2 points | does not |
| "If it's not a good time, no problem" | −3.1 points | not saying it |
| Asks if they want the thing | no difference | asks for a call |
| 40–59 words | +2.7 points | 60–79 words |
| 100 words or more | −2.6 points | 60–79 words |
| Opens "Hi [name], how are you?" | +2.9 points | any other opening |
| Several short messages, lowercase, casual or formal | no difference | one message, standard spelling, neutral |

| Follow-up | Change in interest | Against |
|-----------|--------------------|---------|
| First follow-up: the offer again, in fewer words | +4.9 points | a reminder ("did you see my message?") |
| First follow-up: a new angle, a case or a resource | no difference | a reminder |
| First follow-up: asks for a no ("is it not the moment?") | no more interest, and +10 points of refusals | a reminder |
| Second follow-up: a new angle | +2.2 points | "this is my last message" |
| Second follow-up of 60 words or more | −2.4 points | 15–29 words |

The first follow-up brings 23% of all interested replies, the second 6%.

## Why people said no

A reading of 135 replies classified as not interested or as an objection, from six clients' campaigns, sorted by hand into reasons. A reading, not a measurement: the reasons are our judgment of each reply.

| Reason | Replies |
|--------|---------|
| Not right now, or no reason given | 53 |
| Already have a provider, or do it in-house | 25 |
| Wrong person | 21 |
| Not relevant to their business | 12 |
| Distrust or spam | 4 |
| Other | 20 |
