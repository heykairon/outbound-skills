# Always-On: Reactivation and Nurturing

Every campaign leaves people behind: those who didn't answer, said "not now", or raised an objection. Most teams forget them and keep buying new lists. These two initiatives work the people you already have, and they grow with every campaign you run.

---

## Reactivation

**The bet.** A lead who did not move forward will answer when something changed for them: a new role, news about their company, a post about the problem, a raise. Write again only then, with that change as the reason.

**How it runs.**
- **Park every lead that did not move forward,** with the date and the reason: no answer, "not now", an objection, a lost deal.
- **Watch them for news.** A new role, a company announcement, a post, a funding round.
- **When there is news, write once,** from the person who owns the lead, with the news as the first line and an offer that is new to them. Never send the same sequence again.
- **Wait after a no.** At least 3 months after "not now", and one full sales cycle after a lost deal.

**A real one.** Kairon parked its leads and wrote to them when their company posted news, with the product launch as the new offer:

```
Brandon, it caught my attention that [company] recently posted about how interchange rates can vary across loan portfolios. We're just launching Kairon, and I thought it might be useful to you. [...] Want me to send you access?
```

25 of 229 people messaged replied with interest (11%). Another client reactivated people it had already written to, opening with "a few weeks ago I wrote to you" and offering a new free guide: 5 of 190 (2.6%). The first opened on news about the person, the second on the sender's own earlier message.

## Nurturing

**The bet.** People buy from who they remember. Staying visible to the people who matter, without pitching, keeps you in mind for when they need you.

**How it runs.** A standing campaign over a list, one per tier. Each one checks every person at a set frequency and acts only if they did something, usually a new post:

| Tier | Who | Check | When they post |
|------|-----|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Customers, partners, open opportunities | every day | React, and comment like someone who knows them (each comment approved before it goes out) |
| 2 | Warm leads: past conversations, "not now", objections | every week | React; write only on a real change (a new role, a launch, a raise) |
| 3 | Everyone else who accepted, and old leads | every month | React to their latest post |

A video agency runs it this way: its customers get a reaction and a short, warm comment on every new post, checked every day; its leads from past years get a reaction on posts from the last two weeks, also checked every day.

**What not to do.**
- **No pitch.** A nurturing touch that sells turns into a cold message, and it spends the goodwill the touch was meant to build.
- **No generic comments.** "Great post!" on everything reads like a bot. Comment only when you have something to say about that post.

**How to measure it.** Not by replies: nurturing sends few messages. Track how many of the people in each tier you touched in the period, and how many conversations started from that tier, inbound or after a reactivation.
