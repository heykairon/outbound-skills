# What Worked in Our Clients' Campaigns

These cases come from campaigns run through [Kairon](https://heykairon.com), measured on 2026-11-06. The clients are anonymous. The messages are described, not quoted.

**Interested per contact** is the number of interested replies divided by the number of people contacted (invitations, or messages when there was no invitation). The median client gets about 2%. Many campaigns below reached fewer than 100 people, so read them as signals, not proof.

## The offer decides more than the words

| Client | Offer | Contacted | Interested per contact |
|--------|-------|-----------|------------------------|
| Career-coaching firm | A program to move up in your career, to senior executives | 1,181 | 13.4% |
| Same firm, same message structure | Part-time executives, to small companies | 281 | 0.4% |
| Digital agency | Open jobs, to candidates (long, generic messages asking for the CV) | 41–206 per campaign | 15–39% |
| Recruiting firm | Open jobs, to candidates | 42–89 per campaign | 9–15% |

A job or a career step is something the reader wants for themselves. Those messages broke most of the rules in this skill, and still did best.

## In B2B, a dated event beats the product

| Client | Dated event | Product or content |
|--------|-------------|--------------------|
| Global payroll platform | Small online sessions with peers, with date and time: 6–11% | Messages about the product: 0 interested of 20 |
| Corporate wellbeing firm | Free webinar with a date: 10–12% | Sharing the newsletter: 0 interested of 185 |
| Marketing consultancy | A live talk or workshop with a date: 8–10% | Cold messages by industry with a free review: 0–1% |
| AI consultancy | "We'll have a stand at the tech week in your city": 8.4% | |

## The closer the source, the better

| Source | Example | Interested per contact |
|--------|---------|------------------------|
| The sender's 1st-degree network | Payroll platform inviting contacts to a session | 10.7% |
| People who reacted to a competitor's posts | Our own: founder, free trial with onboarding | 12.5% (80 people) |
| A shared university or employer | Our own: tribes in the US | 13.2% (91 people) |
| Asking for help | Our own: design partners, "something new I'm starting" | 17–22% (40–47 people) |

## Patterns in the words

- **The event, concrete.** What it is, the day, the hour, where, and one question at the end: "Shall I save you a seat?"
- **The follow-up, one line with a new reason.** "I'm closing the list for Tuesday." The weaker campaigns asked "did you see my message?"
- **Named customers and one simple question.** A uniform factory named four hospitals it supplies, then asked who buys the uniforms. 9.2%.
- **The signal beats the biography.** An executive coach opening with six lines of credentials: 4.1%. The same coach naming the event the person organizes: 8.8%.
- **Two or three short messages,** not one block. Most of the best campaigns split the first message.
