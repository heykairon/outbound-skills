# Asking for Help

Use this when the user is looking for product-market fit, for the company or for one feature. The message asks for help, and the frame is discovery. The call can end in a sale, but the message does not sell.

## The Five Parts

1. **Vision.** One sentence on the change the user is trying to make in the reader's world. "I'm trying to make desk rental less of a pain for new businesses." It says what the talk is about, without a product.
2. **Framing.** Where the user is, and what this is. "We're just starting and have nothing to sell yet." A company with products says so, and says it is moving into a new area. The framing lets them say yes without bracing for a pitch.
3. **Weakness.** The thing the user does not understand. "I've only seen it from the tenant's side." A named gap makes the request specific, and people help with a specific gap.
4. **Pedestal.** Why this reader, in particular, can close that gap. "You've rented out desks for years." It must be true of them, so the list decides who gets this message.
5. **Ask.** One clear request for a short talk, with a time frame. Ask for their advice, never for a demo.

The order can move: the framing can come after the pedestal when it reads better there. All five parts go in. Leave out "totally understand if you are busy": in our campaigns, handing the reader the easy no cost interest ([benchmarks](../../initiatives/references/benchmarks.md)).

The rules of the first message in [message-copies](../SKILL.md) still hold: the greeting, 40 to 59 words, one question at the end, no link. Each part is one short sentence:

```
Hi Ana, how are you? I'm trying to make desk rental less of a pain for new businesses. We're just starting, with nothing to sell yet. I've only seen it from the tenant's side. You've rented out desks for years and could help me see the landlord's. Do you have time for a call in the next two weeks?
```

## How to Open the Call

1. **Repeat the vision, the framing and the weakness** in one or two sentences.
2. **Name who introduced you,** if someone did, and why they thought of this person.
3. **Put them on the pedestal again.**
4. **Take the reins with a specific question about their world.** For example: how one of their customers got from where it started to where it is now. Ask about what happened, never about the user's idea.

From there the user steers. Every answer says who has the problem and how they think about it. How to judge those conversations is in **[icp](../../icp/SKILL.md)**, under "No customers yet?".
