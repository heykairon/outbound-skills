# Example: Three Lists That Fill Themselves, and Work

These are lookalike lists our clients run in [Kairon](https://heykairon.com): a Sales Navigator search built from the ICP runs every day, a qualifier judges each result against the ICP, and the ones that fit join a campaign. The clients are anonymous. Measured on 2026-11-09, counting invitations and messages sent at least 7 days before.

---

## A career-coaching firm: one market, split by country and sender

**The ICP.** Senior executives at companies of 201 or more people, with a postgraduate degree and 6 or more years at their current company. The search excludes the functions that are not the buyer: HR, legal, medicine, coaching, recruiting, project and account managers, and general managers. The title list was cut from 129 titles to 83 to keep the search precise.

**The split.** One list per segment, several with their own sender: Argentina; eight Latin American countries; executives who studied at an Argentine university and now live in the United States; and a later list for four countries together.

| List | Passed the ICP | Invitations | Accepted | Messaged | Interested |
|------|----------------|-------------|----------|----------|------------|
| Argentina | 1,334 of 5,524 | 841 | 53% | 418 | 96 (23%) |
| Eight countries | 1,027 of 5,471 | 395 | 44% | 153 | 36 (24%) |
| Argentines in the US | 649 of 3,256 | 566 | 47% | 237 | 53 (22%) |
| Four countries, later | 2,656 of 12,971 | 1,037 | 47% | 473 | 89 (19%) |

Every segment worked. The same firm also ran a lookalike list with a different offer, part-time executives for small companies: 57% accepted, and 1 of 43 people messaged replied with interest. The list machinery was the same; the offer was not.

## A training company: a big market narrowed by exclusions

**The ICP.** Mid-size and large companies in Peru with operational staff spread over many sites (plants, warehouses, stores, mines, sites, fleets), where the buyer is the head of HR or of safety and health.

**The search.** Leaders of HR, safety and health, or training, in Peru. Junior titles and consultants are excluded, and so are the industries with HR leaders but no field staff: banking, financial services, universities, IT services, staffing and non-profits.

**The pace.** 15 people a day, 2 per company, since 20 October.

1,240 returned, 285 passed the ICP (23%). 235 invitations, 54% accepted. 16 of 113 people messaged replied with interest (14%).

## A content agency: one rule decides who fits

**The ICP.** Marketing and growth leaders in Latin America, at companies that grow through marketing. The rule that decides it: the company's average ticket per customer is under USD 5,000.

**The search.** Leaders of marketing, growth, performance, acquisition, e-commerce or brand. It excludes founders and CEOs, and the B2B words that point to high-ticket companies: demand generation, ABM, account-based, enterprise, partnerships, business development.

**The pace.** 25 people a day, 2 per company, since 26 October.

2,400 returned, 355 passed the ICP (15%). 140 invitations, 41% accepted. 5 of 33 people messaged replied with interest (15%), still a small number.

## What the three show

- **Exclude before you add.** All three searches carry long exclusion lists: the functions, titles and industries that look like the buyer and are not.
- **Split it.** The coaching firm learned each segment's rate separately.
- **The list and the offer are separate tests.** The same firm, with the same kind of list, got 1 interested reply of 43 with a different offer.
- **Watch acceptance early.** In three other lookalike lists, acceptance stayed under 30%, and they produced 1 interested reply among 77 people messaged. A rule of thumb from these lists: if the first hundred invitations accept under 30%, fix the search before you fill the list.
