# Example: Three Small Markets, Worked Account by Account

These are three of our clients' markets, worked in [Kairon](https://heykairon.com). The clients are anonymous. Measured on 2026-11-09. Each account is a company; a person counts as reached when they got an invitation or a message.

---

## A consumer fintech tool: build the whole market, then stay visible

**The market.** Consumer fintechs in Latin America with 11 to 1,000 employees and a support team of 3 or more. Saved as one list of 1,280 companies, then narrowed to 326 that fit.

**The buyers.** Several people per company.

**What they did.** A warm-up before every invitation, and no message yet: 565 people invited across 248 accounts, 184 accepted (33%). 82 accounts have 3 or more people reached.

This is steps 1, 2 and 4 of the skill: the whole market saved, several buyers mapped per account, and the market made aware of the sender before anyone is pitched. The results that matter, the conversations, are still ahead. [open: add the account states after the first messages go out.]

## A textile factory: one market, split by the type of buyer

**The market.** Health institutions in Argentina that buy uniforms, sheets and scrubs: 530 saved, split into public hospitals (62), private clinics and sanatoriums (132), and diagnostic and outpatient centers (287).

**The buyers.** Purchasing, housekeeping and linen, and HR for uniforms. Each segment has its own list of people.

**What happened.** 187 accounts reached, with 330 people. In 22 of them (11.8%), someone replied with interest. Per person, public hospitals did best: 5 of 38 people reached, against 7 of 129 at private clinics.

| People reached in the account | Accounts | Some reply | Someone interested |
|-------------------------------|----------|------------|--------------------|
| 1 | 135 | 21% | 7% |
| 3–4 | 18 | 56% | 39% |

## An integrations platform for banks: tiers and depth

**The market.** Banks, payment processors, fintechs and retailers in Latin America, saved as lists by country and by tier: a shortlist of 25 named accounts in Mexico, a second tier of 566 companies with 200 or more employees, and a list of the accounts already reached before a trip to Mexico.

**What happened.** 1,368 accounts reached, with 5,852 people. In 99 accounts (7.2%), someone replied with interest.

| People reached in the account | Accounts | Some reply | Someone interested |
|-------------------------------|----------|------------|--------------------|
| 1 | 725 | 10% | 2.8% |
| 2 | 191 | 16% | 4.7% |
| 3–4 | 162 | 27% | 9.9% |
| 5 or more | 290 | 49% | 18.6% |

Much of the interest came from reasons to meet: a trip to Mexico and a webinar.

## What the three show

- **Reach more than one person per account.** In both markets that reached people in depth, accounts with 3 or more people reached were several times more likely to have someone interested. Part of that is arithmetic, since more people means more chances, and part is that the best-fit accounts got mapped deeper. Read it as a reason to map 3 to 5 buyers, not as a rate to expect.
- **Split the market by the type of buyer.** The textile factory's segments drew interest at different rates, which a single list would have hidden.
- **Save the market before you write.** The fintech tool knows its market is 326 companies, so it can measure how much of it is reached. A list that only grows from searches cannot.
