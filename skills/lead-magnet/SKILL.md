# Lead Magnet

A lead magnet is something free that solves one narrow problem completely, and by solving it, shows the next problem: the one the paid offer solves. Hormozi's picture, from *$100M Leads*: the bar gives away salty pretzels, and the pretzels make you thirsty. The pretzels are free. The drinks are not.

This skill owns `.agents/lead-magnets.md`: what a lead magnet is made of, and its rules. Other skills read the file. They never define a lead magnet themselves.

## Before Starting

**Requires [value-prop](../value-prop/SKILL.md).** Read that skill's Before Starting first, and read the files it names. If the file that skill owns does not exist yet, do that skill first. This skill does not repeat any of it.

A lead magnet is for one ICP, and it starts from that ICP's problems and solutions. Read `.agents/lead-magnets.md` if it exists, and update only what changed.

Draft from what you hold, then ask, one thing per turn.

---

## 1. Try the Paid Offer First

Hormozi's own advice: offering the core product might be all you need, so try that first. A lead magnet is an extra step between a stranger and the sale. Add it only when the direct ask does not work.

Our data points the same way. Within the same organization, first messages that offered a free thing drew fewer interested replies than messages that asked for the call. Those things were mostly generic: a report, a guide. A lead magnet made for the person is a different bet, and an untested one. So if the user has never sent the direct ask to this ICP, say so, and propose testing it next to the lead magnet.

---

## 2. Pick One Narrow Problem

Take the problems and solutions list in `.agents/value-prop.md`. Pick **one** problem where:
- it is narrow enough to solve completely for free;
- solving it shows the next problem, and the paid offer solves that one;
- the buyer already feels it. They don't need to be taught that it exists.

**Test:** after the lead magnet, does the buyer want more? If it solves everything, nobody needs the paid offer. If it solves nothing, nobody trusts the paid offer.

---

## 3. Choose the Type and the Form

Three types:
- **Reveal a problem.** Show them a problem they have and didn't see, or didn't measure: an audit, a diagnosis, a score.
- **A sample.** A small piece of the paid product, for free.
- **One step of many.** Do the first step of a process. The paid offer does the rest.

Four forms: a **tool** (a calculator, a template, software), **information** (a report, a list, a video), a **service** (you do the work for them), or something **physical**. Three types by four forms gives twelve options for the same problem. Draft at least three, and recommend one.

---

## 4. In Outbound, Make It for the Person

A stranger on LinkedIn will not spend time on a generic guide. Make one real unit of the paid work, for this person, from public facts: their website, their posts, their job ads, their reviews, their competitors. Done for them beats done by them.

Hormozi tells this story about his gym business. For four months, his cold outreach offered a free "game plan session", which was a sales call. When he swapped it for real service, as much as he could afford to give, the take rate tripled. A free call is not a lead magnet: the buyer knows it is a pitch.

**When the problem needs their own data** (their invoices, their numbers), pick another problem that public facts can solve. If none can, ask for the smallest piece of data, only after they say yes, and say plainly that it costs them effort.

Two rules:
- **Every fact about the person is true and checked.** One wrong fact about their company and the lead magnet proves you don't know them. Never invent a number, a competitor or a problem. Leave the gap open.
- **The recipe repeats.** Write down where each fact comes from and how the piece is built, so the user can make one for each person on the list without a day of work for each.

---

## 5. Make It Worth Paying For, and Free of Effort

- **As good as paid.** Give away the secrets, sell the implementation. If the user would be embarrassed to charge for it, it is not good enough to give away.
- **Nothing to do to get it.** No form, no call, no login. A short message, a one-page file or a short video they can read on the phone.
- **Easy to use.** They see the result in the first few seconds. Put the finding first, then the detail.

---

## 6. Name It

Hormozi tests the name first: the name decides more than the content whether they take it. In a LinkedIn message, the name is how the offer is said. Write three ways to say it, concrete and in the buyer's words. "Your 5 oldest unpaid invoices, and who to call first" beats "a collections diagnosis".

---

## 7. The Next Step

Say what to do next, and give a reason to do it now. Hormozi: any reason works better than none. The reason must be true: "I'm doing five this month", only if they really make five. Never a fake deadline or a fake scarcity.

In a first message, offer it. Don't send it yet. Ask if they want it, and send it when they say yes. In our data, a link in the first message drew fewer interested replies than no link. Once they have it, the next step is the paid offer: usually the call about the next problem.

## The File

```markdown
# Lead Magnets

_Last updated: YYYY-MM-DD_

## [Short name]
- **For:** [ICP name, as in icp.md]
- **Status:** [testing since YYYY-MM-DD | validated, with how many were offered it and how many were interested per 100]
- **Problem it solves:** [one problem from value-prop.md, in their words]
- **Next problem it shows:** [the one the paid offer solves]
- **Type and form:** [reveal a problem | a sample | one step of many] + [tool | information | service | physical]
- **What they get:** [the concrete thing, and how long it takes them to use it]
- **How we make it per person:** [where each fact comes from, the steps, the time it takes]
- **How we say it:** [the three ways to name it]
- **Next step:** [what we ask for after, and the true reason to do it now]
```

One block per lead magnet. Which one each initiative uses lives in **[initiatives](../initiatives/SKILL.md)**.
